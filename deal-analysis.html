Questions buyers ask

What is a deal analysis?

A written review of one specific listing before you make an offer: the comparable transaction range for its industry, whether the add-backs hold up, the financing structure the numbers support, and the maximum price the deal covers. Due diligence on the numbers, delivered in two business days.

How is this different from the free calculator or the workbook?

Those use rules-of-thumb ranges and your inputs. This is prepared by an advisor using licensed comparable transaction data for the target's specific industry, and includes a professional read on the add-backs instead of taking the listing at face value.

Is this a formal valuation or appraisal?

No. It is an advisor's opinion of the market range and structure, meant to inform an offer and a lender conversation. If your SBA lender requires a formal appraisal, they will order one from a certified appraiser — and this report will make that conversation shorter.

What do you need from me?

The listing or offering memorandum, the last two to three years of financials if you have them, the asking price, and how you plan to finance it. The listing alone is enough to start.

What if the deal is bad?

Then the report says so, and you have spent a fraction of what diligence on a bad deal costs. Roughly a third of the listings we review are priced above what the earnings support. That is not a failure of the analysis; it is the point of it.

Who does this?

XP M&A Advisory. We work with acquirers on the buy side and hold licensed access to the transaction databases lenders and appraisers rely on. No individual names on the page, on purpose — the process is the same whoever runs it.
